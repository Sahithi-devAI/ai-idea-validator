## Scoring Rubrics

Score each dimension 1–5 using these criteria exactly. Do not cluster scores around 3.
Use 1–2 when the evidence warrants it.

### Market Potential
Score on two signals: **Problem Prevalence** (how many people face this regularly?) and **Switching Readiness** (are they actively seeking a solution, or have they resigned to the status quo?).

- **5** — Problem is widespread AND people are actively seeking solutions (search interest, communities, workarounds exist in the wild)
- **4** — Widespread problem, meaningful demand signals, some incumbents but a real wedge exists
- **3** — Proven market, real problem, competitive — entrants can win with differentiation but need to earn it
- **2** — Problem is real but affects a small or hard-to-reach group, OR large group with near-zero urgency, OR market owned by an aggregator with distribution moat (Google, Apple, Microsoft, Salesforce, etc.)
- **1** — No clear market, theoretical problem, or this is squarely on an incumbent's near-term roadmap

Do NOT score market potential based on category hype (e.g. "AI agents is a large market"). Score based on observable evidence of the specific problem.

### Product Usefulness
This is the MOST IMPORTANT dimension. Weight it heavily.

- **5** — Solves a frequent, painful problem with no good existing alternative. Users would be "very disappointed" if it disappeared (Sean Ellis threshold). Hair-on-fire problem.
- **4** — Clear recurring pain. Existing solutions are meaningfully worse. Users would miss it but could awkwardly work around it.
- **3** — Real but moderate pain. Alternatives exist and are adequate. Nice-to-have that some users actively want.
- **2** — Infrequent use case, or the improvement over alternatives is marginal. Most users wouldn't notice it was gone.
- **1** — Vague problem definition, or people have already adapted to this pain, or the product addresses a symptom not the real underlying need.

### Feasibility & Defensibility
Score on two components: can it be built reliably, and can it be sustained?

- **5** — Technically proven or low-risk to build. Clear durable moat: data flywheel, network effects, or deep workflow lock-in.
- **4** — Achievable with manageable risk. Some real edge: proprietary data, integration depth, or first-mover in a defined niche.
- **3** — Achievable, technically undifferentiated. Moat is execution-only — copyable within 12 months by a well-funded competitor.
- **2** — Significant platform dependency risk (platform owner can ship this natively), OR technically complex with unproven reliability at scale.
- **1** — Relies on technology that doesn't reliably work yet, OR hardware bet without software validation first, OR directly in a platform giant's roadmap.

### Monetization Viability
Score on whether value capture aligns with value delivery.

- **5** — Proven willingness to pay in adjacent products. Clear B2B/prosumer pricing analog. High LTV, incentives align with user outcomes.
- **4** — Monetizable with some friction. Clear budget line item exists for this category. Pricing model is obvious.
- **3** — Monetizable but requires behavior change or user education. Consumer subscription with uncertain retention.
- **2** — Consumer freemium with a weak conversion path, OR monetization requires scale that doesn't exist yet.
- **1** — No clear monetization path, purely ad-supported, or the value sits in a layer this product doesn't own.

## Build Signal

Assign ONE signal:
**strong_yes | cautious_yes | not_recommended**

Signal calibration:
- **strong_yes** — Usefulness ≥ 4 AND average across all dimensions ≥ 3.5
- **cautious_yes** — Usefulness ≥ 3 AND average ≥ 3.0 AND no dimension scores below 2
- **not_recommended** — Usefulness ≤ 2, OR two or more dimensions score 1, OR average < 2.5

If the idea falls between cautious_yes and not_recommended, apply the usefulness gate: usefulness below 3 means not_recommended regardless of other scores.

Also provide:
- Weakest dimension
- Sharp 1-line verdict
